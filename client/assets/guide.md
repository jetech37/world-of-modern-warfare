# Welcome
## Welcome to World of Modern Warfare
This is World of Warcraft 1.12 with Modern Warfare 2 on top: guns, perks, killstreaks, kits and more, all inside Azeroth.
Everything from classic WoW is still here: quests, dungeons, professions, your class and its spells. The modern layer adds a first-person shooter way to play it.
## The keys to remember
- {WOMW_SWITCH} switches between Modern (your guns and gear) and Classic (melee, spells and your class abilities). {WOMW_SHEATHE} puts your weapons away for your Hands (Utility), and again draws them back.
- Hold Left Alt to get your mouse cursor back for the interface. Press Ctrl+Alt to keep it out, and Ctrl+Alt again to aim.
## Reopening this guide
Type /help or /womw in chat, or press Esc and pick Guide.
## Changing keys
Every key of ours can be changed: press Esc, pick Key Bindings, and look under World of Modern Warfare.
## Moving windows
Drag a window by its title to move it; it opens there next time too. The small round arrow at its top right puts it back.

## The world map
- M (or the Map button on the menu bar) opens the map as a window you can keep open while you play. Hold Left Alt to use it with the mouse; Esc or M closes it.
- Drag its frame to move it; drag the gold grip at its bottom right (or Ctrl + mouse wheel) to size it. The round arrow by its close button puts it back.
- It fades while you move and comes back when you stop or point at it. Your coordinates and the cursor's show along its bottom.
- Click a zone to zoom in, right-click to zoom out; the mouse wheel does both. The dropdowns pick a continent and zone.
- The map's Options button and Esc, Options, Interface hold its settings, including Full-Screen World Map for the classic map.

# Controls
## Your three action bars
- Modern, Classic and Utility each have their own action bar, and only the one you are using shows. Each page has 6 slots: {WOMW_SLOT1}, {WOMW_SLOT2}, {WOMW_SLOT3}, {WOMW_SLOT4}, {WOMW_SLOT5} and {WOMW_SLOT6}.
- Hold Alt and turn the mouse wheel for the bar's other pages. The number beside the bar shows which bar and page you are on (M1 is Modern's first).
- Drag things around a bar to arrange it. Each ability only goes on its own bar; your bars are saved with your character.
## Shooting (Modern)
- {WOMW_FIRE}: fire. {WOMW_AIM}: aim down sights.
- {WOMW_RELOAD}: reload. An empty magazine reloads by itself when you pull the trigger.
- The Modern bar has your primary and secondary gun, the underbarrel attachment, your killstreak, your tactical and lethal grenades, and on its second page special ammo and rearm. The mouse wheel swaps guns too.
- Hold a lethal grenade's slot key to cook a frag (the fuse is ticking!), let go to throw. With C4, press it again to set it off.
- Left Shift (hold): sprint. You can't shoot while sprinting, and sprint runs out after a few seconds.
## Moving
- Tap Left Ctrl to crouch, hold it to go prone (hold again to stand). Space rises a stance.
- Space: stand up one stance, or jump when standing. Space while running at a ledge climbs it.
- {WOMW_VIEW}: first or third person.
## Other keys
- {WOMW_INTERACT}: use. Talk to NPCs, open doors, loot, pick up a gun from the ground, use a revival kit.
- Tab: target the nearest enemy. H: health bars over heads.
- J: your character. {WOMW_KITS}: your kits, with their Talents and Abilities pages. L: your quest log. P: social. M: the map.
- {WOMW_COMPANIONS}: your companions. {WOMW_BUILD}: the Construction window (building).
- Your free-for-all PvP flag is a check box on the Honor tab of your character window. It turns on at once and off after 5 minutes out of combat.

# Hands and spells
## Melee weapons and spells in your hands
- {WOMW_SWITCH} puts the gun away and raises your hands: the Classic bar shows your class spells and abilities. {WOMW_SWITCH} again brings the gun back.
- Left mouse swings or casts with the right hand, right mouse with the left hand. A shield on the left raises to block while you hold right mouse.
- {WOMW_SHEATHE} puts everything away: you fight with your bare fists.
## Putting a spell in a hand
- Press a spell's key (or click it on the bar) to put it in your right hand. Hold Alt with its key, or right-click it, for the left hand.
- Some spells charge: hold the mouse button, then let go to cast where you aim. Letting go too early fizzles it without spending anything.
- The same spell in both hands, let go together, casts one stronger blast.
- Hold Alt as you let go to cast on yourself.
- Buffs, shouts and stances cast at once. "Next swing" abilities like Heroic Strike ride on your next swing.
- Swinging a melee weapon never dashes you forward. Warriors close the distance with Charge.
## Bolts
- Fireball, Frostbolt, Shadow Bolt and the like fly from your hand where you aim, with no range limit: they keep going until they hit someone, a wall or the ground. Bushes and anything else you can walk through do not stop them.
- A bolt does not need a direct hit. Enemies near where it lands take a share: about 75% right next to it, down to 50% at the edge. A direct hit does full damage. Walls and cover protect from the splash.
- Crowd control like Polymorph or Fear only works on a direct hit. Slows and damage over time also reach nearby enemies, weaker.
- Bolts fly straight. Some spells home in: Arcane Missiles curves toward your selected target, or the enemy nearest your crosshair. It turns at a limited rate, so a nimble target can still dodge it.
- Some spells fade out after a set distance, with a small puff: Arcane Missiles after 45 yards, the shamans' shocks after 30, and instant spells such as Mind Blast at twice their usual range.
## Sprays
- Mages have two sprays, like a flamethrower: Scorch is a stream of fire and Cone of Cold a spray of frost. Both are on the bar from level 1.
- Hold the mouse button to keep spraying. There is no charge. Every enemy in the cone in front of you takes damage several times a second. The frost spray also slows them.
- A spray costs mana for as long as you hold it, about 16 seconds from a full pool. It stops when you let go, run out of mana, or are silenced.
## Druids
In a shapeshift form you have no gun: the slot keys use the form's own bar (Alt and the wheel for its other half) and left mouse swipes.
## Rogues
- A rogue picks from three archetypes at each tier: the Assassin, the Trickster and the Duelist. (The Bard is gone: music is a profession now. A former Bard is a Duelist, with the same tier picks.)
- Assassin: open the fight from stealth. Ambush (from behind), Cheap Shot and Garrote only work from stealth or in the 4 seconds after you leave it, the Opening, when your blades and abilities hit twice as hard. Distract turns enemies away so Ambush works from the front; Preparation readies Vanish for another opening.
- Trickster: Deadly Poison and the Poison Satchel (press it again to switch between Crippling, Mind-numbing and Wound Poison) coat your blades. Smoke Bomb puts you back into stealth even in a fight. Light Fingers lifts a purse into your bags, in combat too, and makes Pick Lock instant. Every Trickster ability makes you untargetable for a moment.
- Duelist: fight in the open with both hands. Every melee blow adds Momentum (the buff over your bar): each point adds 5% to your melee damage, and it ebbs when you stop swinging. Lunge is the one dash; Riposte parries blows from in front and strikes back; Blade Flurry hits a second enemy too.
- Lockpicks open chests, lockboxes and doors of the world, never a player's homestead door or portal.

# Utility and gathering
## Your tools in your hands
- {WOMW_SHEATHE} puts your weapons away: the Utility bar shows your Hands and the trade tools you can use. Press a tool's slot key to hold it; press Hands to put it away and fight with your fists again.
- The tools are your professions' own: a Mining Pick, a Skinning Knife, your hands for Herbalism, a Fishing Pole, a Woodcutting Axe, a Blacksmith Hammer and the Repair Hammer. A tool shows on the bar only while you have it (in your bags or equipped; Herbalism needs none) and know its trade (the Repair Hammer only needs owning). Pick one up or learn the trade and its button appears; sell it or unlearn the trade and the button goes.
- Tools only go on the Utility bar.
## Gathering
- Aim at an ore vein, a herb, a lumber tree or a skinnable corpse and press {WOMW_FIRE} to swing. Hold {WOMW_FIRE} to keep swinging.
- A vein or a tree takes 2 to 4 swings, a herb or a corpse 1 to 3: fewer the more your skill is above what it asks. The last swing gathers, with the usual loot window and skill-ups.
- The number under your crosshair shows how far along you are. Stop for a few seconds, or swing at something else, and you start over.
## Fishing
- With the Fishing Pole, {WOMW_FIRE} casts the line ahead of you into the water. When the bobber bites, press {WOMW_FIRE} again to reel it in.
## Smithing
- With the Blacksmith Hammer, strike an anvil or a forge to open your Blacksmithing window.

## Minstrelsy
- Minstrelsy is music as a profession, for every class, and it takes no profession slot. A Minstrel Trainer stands by the innkeeper of every capital and of Goldshire, the Crossroads, Booty Bay and Gadgetzan: learn it there (Apprentice, then Journeyman, Expert and Artisan, up to skill 300) and buy a starter Hand Drum. Every innkeeper sells the Hand Drum too.
- The instruments are tools: the Hand Drum (skill 1), the Lute (50), the Flute (100), the Harp (150), the Bagpipes (225) and the Thunder Drums (275). One shows on the Utility bar once you carry it and your skill reaches it. Minstrels make their own: learn each instrument's recipe from the trainer when your skill reaches it, then make it anywhere from the Minstrelsy window, from other trades' goods (lumber, leather, thread, bars). Making one teaches Minstrelsy too, in the usual colours. The Thunder Drums are learned only from their score, which the trolls' drummers and casters rarely drop.
- Ready an instrument with its slot key and press it again to pick the next song; the line under your crosshair names it. Fire plays it, fire again stops it.
- A note highway rises over your Utility bar, one lane over each key the instrument uses. After four ticks of the metronome, press a lane's key as its note reaches the gold line: Perfect, Good or Miss. Hold the key for a long note, press several together for a chord. Hits in a row raise your combo, up to x4. Keep moving while you play.
- The better you play, the stronger the song and the faster your skill rises. A song teaches while it is orange, yellow or green to you (see the Minstrelsy window; bind its key in Key Bindings), and a nearly perfect one teaches two points.
- Travel songs make your party run faster out of combat. Inn songs, played at an inn or in a city, give everyone listening rested experience, once each half hour. Fire songs, played by a campfire, make the party regenerate out of combat and their other songs last longer. From skill 225 three combat songs give small boosts: a little less damage taken, a little more speed, a little regeneration.
- Only combat songs can be played in a fight; a fight, a stun, a silence or a fear, or switching your hands stops any other song.
- People listening may tip you: a line with tip buttons shows after a song you hear (Alt frees the cursor). Tips are capped each day, and never between your own characters. A good song at an inn may earn coin from the crowd as well, up to a small amount each day.

# Progression
## Unlocks and achievements
Every account starts with only the USP .45. Every other gun, attachment, camo, perk, grenade and killstreak is unlocked by earning achievements.
Open your achievements from the menu bar, or the Achievements tab of your quest log (L): your kills, deaths and best streak, every achievement with its progress and what it unlocks, and the Prestige tab.
## Kits (Create-a-Class)
- Press {WOMW_KITS} to see your kits: guns, attachments, perks, grenades, talents and killstreaks. You can keep up to 5.
- The kit's name at the top is a dropdown: pick another kit, or Rename, New Kit, Copy Kit and Delete Kit (which asks first).
- On the Loadout page, click a part of the kit on the left (Primary, an attachment, a perk...). The right side lists every choice for it with what it does, its numbers next to your current pick and what unlocks it. Click one, then Assign to Kit.
- The arrow keys work too: up and down pick the part, left and right the choice, Enter assigns it. Then Save & Equip.
- To change or switch kits, talk to any class trainer or innkeeper and pick "Edit your kits".
- Anywhere else, the kit window is for looking, except for a short field edit once an hour.
- Trainers and innkeepers rearm you for free: full ammo and equipment. Their "Rearm" option also tops up special rounds for a small fee.
## Class abilities
- Every class ability exists once, like a gun: there are no ranks to train. It grows stronger with your level by itself, and your bars and macros keep working as it does.
- The Abilities page ({WOMW_ABILITIES}) lists each ability once, with its icon. Its tooltip shows the numbers at your level. Drag one onto an action button to place it.
- Class trainers sell your class options, once each. They never sell ranks.
- Most abilities are limited by their cooldown alone. Three archetypes keep a resource, shown as a buff over your action bar with its count: Holy Power (Crusader paladins), Static (Elementalist shamans) and Life Force (Necromancer warlocks). Rest the pointer on the buff to see what fills and spends it.
## Bounty boards
Most towns and outposts of the open-world zones have a Bounty Board. Right-click it to see the bounties posted for that zone today.
- Each bounty asks for something that really lives in the zone: kill a number of a creature, collect a drop, clear a camp, or hunt down a named rare or elite for a bigger purse.
- Pick one in the list to read it, then Accept. It goes in your quest log and the quest tracker like any quest.
- Turn it in at any board in the same zone (Complete Bounty) for experience and gold for its level, and reputation with the board's town.
- You can finish 5 bounties a day. The boards post new ones every morning (server time); a bounty you took runs until the end of the next day.
- Bounties count toward the Bounty Hunter achievements: a title at 10, a camo for every gun at 50, and another title and camo at 100.
## Wanderers
Wanderers are people of the playable races who live in the open world: the name over their head has a trade under it, like Fisherman of Lakeshire or Miner of Goldshire.
- Each has a home, a place of work and a market. A fisherman fishes Lake Everstill and walks to Stormwind to sell his catch; a minstrel plays the inns in the evening; a courier carries parcels from town to town. They sleep at night, eat at inns and take a day off now and then.
- Right-click one to talk: ask about their life, where they are headed, the news, or rumours (a bounty posted, a rare creature seen).
- Let me see your wares: what they sell is what they carry, gathered or bought on their rounds. Now and then that includes something rare.
- Walk with me a while: pay one to travel with you for ten minutes. They fight what attacks you. Cowards and those who dislike you refuse.
- They remember you: who greeted them, who helped them in a fight, who traded with them and who hurt them. Friends get better prices.
- Some are brave and fight anything; others run and cry for help. Help one in trouble and they will thank you by name.
## Killstreaks
Get kills in a row without dying. Pick three rewards in your kit, then use the killstreak slot on your Modern bar to call one in. Earned rewards wait for you even if you die.
Some have Azeroth versions:
- Goblin Rocket: you steer it down from the sky with the mouse; click to light its second stage, Esc to bail out. Type /rocketsens and a number from 0.2 to 3 to change how fast it turns.
- Dwarven Sentry Turret: set it down within six yards. It covers its front for a minute and a half, turning first on whoever is fighting you.
- Sky Rider Gunship and Gyrocopter Gunship fly in from the horizon and circle you for a minute. Gnomish Sky Hornets and the Siege Bomber bomb a line across your mark.
- Your craft, their time left, their health and their kills show at the top left, under the UAV.
- Everyone near a bombing run sees where its bombs will land a few seconds before they do. Red marks are an enemy's: get clear.
- Enemy craft and turrets can be shot down for credit: guns and spells hurt them, launchers and grenades most.
## Perks and deathstreaks
Perks change how you play (Windrunner's Stride for endless sprint, Mercenary Contract for cheaper killstreaks, and more). Deathstreaks help you when you keep dying.
- Any three perks, from any tier. Some stack when you take them again: each Tinker's Rails lets your primary carry one more attachment, up to four with three Rails. A gun takes one of each kind (one sight, one underbarrel, one muzzle...), and only combinations the gun really has: the kit window greys out the rest and says why. Your secondary's second attachment comes with Tinker's Rails Pro. The gun shows two of them (sights and underbarrels first), and all of them count.
- Every perk has a Pro version. Get kills with the perk in your kit to earn its Pro (see Achievements), then pick Regular or Pro for it in the kit window.

# Companions
## Getting a companion
Creatures sometimes drop a companion item. Right-click it in your bags to bring that companion out.
Every pet of the old world is a companion too: the cats, parrots, snakes and frogs from pet vendors, the whelplings, the Mechanical Squirrel, Mr. Wiggles and the holiday pets. Each one is the first form of its line and levels, learns moves and evolves like any other. An old pet item you already had has become its companion item.
## Fighting together
- Your companion uses the normal pet frame. Its moves, ride and commands are on the Companion bar: press C to show it over your hands' bar (C, X or Z goes back).
- {PETBAR} use its Companion bar slots, aimed at your crosshair, even with your gun up.
## Fainting and reviving
When a companion is beaten it faints. Only that one: your other companions can still come out. A fainted companion can't be summoned or ridden until it is revived:
- Talk to any stable master and pick "Revive and heal my companions". It costs a few coins by level and heals every wound too.
- Use a Reviving Draught, sold by general goods vendors and stable masters. Right-click the draught, or right-click the fainted companion's item twice. It comes back at half health.
- Or wait about an hour, and it comes round on its own, even while you are logged out.
Losing a companion duel never makes it faint. The Companions window and the item's tooltip show how long a fainted companion still needs.
## The Companions window
The Companions button on the menu bar ({WOMW_COMPANIONS}) opens the window. Drag it by its title or background; it stays where you put it, and the round arrow at its top right puts it back in the middle.
- The Companions tab: every companion in your bags as a card, with its health, its experience and what it is waiting for ("Ready to evolve!", "New move to learn!", fainted). Pick one to see it turn in 3D (drag the picture, or the arrows under it), its moves with their icons, its traits, its evolution path and what its next form needs. Summon, Ride, Evolve and Use Reviving Draught are there too.
- The Bestiary tab: every kind of companion by family. Target a creature to add its entry; carry one to mark it caught. An entry shows the creature in 3D (click a form on its evolution path to see that one), where it is found, its moves and the traits it may have.
Talk to a stable master for the full window: reviving, breeding and changing a stage's move.

# Building
## Build mode
- {WOMW_BUILD}: open or close the Construction window. Pick a piece there, or press its Build button, to enter build mode: your gun goes away and the hammer comes out.
- Esc leaves build mode. In build mode, {WOMW_BUILD} brings the Construction window back for another piece.
- {WOMW_BUILDPREV} and {WOMW_BUILDNEXT}: previous and next piece. The mouse wheel moves it nearer or further. {WOMW_RELOAD} rotates it.
- {WOMW_FIRE} places a piece, {WOMW_DECONSTRUCT} takes one of yours down, {WOMW_AIM} reinforces your own piece.
- Pieces cost lumber, stone and smiths' parts, and need Construction skill (learn it from a Construction Trainer). Building is expensive: a wood wall is 16 Rough Lumber and 4 Copper Nails, a stone wall 20 Solid Stone and 5 Iron Brackets. There is no limit on how many pieces you own.
## Decay and repair
- Out in the world every piece wears down by 2% of its health a day: one left alone falls after about 50 days. Pieces on your own plot don't decay while its upkeep is paid.
- Aim at a piece to see its health and how many days it has left (in build mode, or with the Repair Hammer in your hands).
- The Repair Hammer mends pieces. Buy one from a construction supplier, or a smith can make it. Hold it in Utility, aim at a damaged piece and hold {WOMW_FIRE}: each strike mends a tenth of it.
- Each strike uses a share of the piece's own materials (fresh or salvaged; mending a whole piece costs about 60% of building it). A piece hit in the last 30 seconds can't be mended. Repairs never raise your Construction skill.
- You can mend your own pieces, those on a plot that lets you build, and those of your party.
## Salvage
- Taking a piece down gives back about half its materials as "Salvaged" items. They build pieces just like fresh ones, but only pieces built from fresh materials can raise your Construction skill.
## Doors and portals
- {WOMW_INTERACT} opens a door. In build mode, {WOMW_INTERACT} on your own door sets its lock: just you, your party (the default), your guild, a code of 4 to 8 digits, or anyone.
- A door with a code shows a keypad. Too many wrong codes and it makes you wait.
- At Construction 275 you can build a portal (rare materials, two per player). {WOMW_INTERACT} on it takes you to your faction's capitals or your homestead. It shares your hearthstone's cooldown.
## Your own plot
- Buy a plot with gold from a plot steward. Couriers by the blacksmiths in Stormwind and Orgrimmar (and the flight masters in the other capitals) can take you there.
- Plots have a weekly upkeep: the first week is included and you can prepay up to 12 weeks.
- If upkeep runs out you get two weeks of grace. After that, or if you release the plot, your pieces come down as if taken down: about half, salvaged, waits in your reclaim stash at the plot steward.
- Releasing a plot, and anything else at the plot steward that can't be undone, asks you to confirm first.
- One plot per account. Only you, people you allow, and GMs can touch it. People you allow open every door on it.
- The courier's "Take me back" shares your hearthstone's cooldown.
## Furniture
- The Construction window's Furniture tab: chairs, beds, tables, chests, bookshelves, crafting stations, banners, rugs, lights and more. Make them at the Construction trade window like any piece; the Plot Steward sells a few (the mailbox, the training dummy, banners), and you sometimes find one when cutting lumber.
- Furniture snaps to a quarter-yard grid and {WOMW_RELOAD} turns it 15 degrees. Hold Sprint (Shift) to place it freely. Small things (candles, mugs, books) go on tables; banners, shields, paintings and trophies hang on walls.
- {WOMW_INTERACT} uses a piece: sit in a chair, lie in a bed, read a bookshelf, open a chest.
- A bed on your homestead lets you rest there as at an inn. Chests, footlockers and wardrobes share one stash per plot, open to you and those you permit.
- An anvil, forge, cooking fire, loom or alchemy table works for your professions within a few yards. A mailbox works as any mailbox (right-click it). Hit a training dummy to see your damage per second. With a companion out, use a trophy plinth to show it off. Songs played on a bard's stage land a quarter stronger.

The Construction button on the menu bar ({WOMW_BUILD}) shows your skill, your plot and what you can build.

# Looting and survival
## Area looting
Out of combat, opening one corpse shows the loot of every corpse near you in one window, sorted by corpse. Anything that doesn't fit stays on its corpse for next time. In combat, each corpse loots on its own.
## Ammo
Ammo is limited. Out of combat your reserve trickles back to one magazine. For a full load go back to any trainer or innkeeper: talking to one rearms you for free, as does Rearm on your Modern bar while resting in a city or inn. Gunners and enemy players drop rounds when they die, and a dungeon boss restocks everyone who fought it. The Gravedigger's Bandolier perk takes twice the rounds from the fallen and a magazine for every gun each kill; the Field Quartermaster perk lets Rearm work anywhere every 10 minutes.
## Going down
- A killing blow leaves you downed instead of dead. You crawl, and bleed out after 20 to 60 seconds.
- A resurrection spell or a revival kit brings you back: First Aid's Field Revival Kit or Engineering's Gnomish Revival Coil. Press {WOMW_INTERACT} to use one on yourself, or on a downed friend under your crosshair.
## Picking up guns
Enemy gunners and players drop their gun. Look at it and hold {WOMW_INTERACT} to take it until you die, swap kits or log out.
