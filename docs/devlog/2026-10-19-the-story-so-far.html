The story so far, and going open source

World of Modern Warfare (WOMW) started with a simple question: what if the classic world of Azeroth played like a modern first-person shooter? Not a minigame bolted onto the side, but the whole thing: you walk the same zones, take the same quests and meet the same creatures, only now you see it through your own eyes, aim with the mouse and fight with a modern arsenal.

This is the first post of the devlog. It covers what has been built so far, what we learned from the first playtest, and the next big step: the project is going open source.

How it works

WOMW has three parts that grew up together:

  • The server is a fork of vmangos, the open-source 1.12 server. All of the shooter's rules live in a library of their own, with its own tests: guns, hit detection, kits, perks, killstreaks, companions, construction. The game server only wires them in.
  • The client is built on benilla, a from-scratch 1.12 client written in Rust and Bevy. Our layer on top of it adds first person, the guns, the HUD and every new window.
  • The launcher installs the game, signs you up, keeps you up to date and starts it.

The server referees everything. When you fire, your client sends the shot; the server checks it against its own copy of the world, rewound to the moment you pulled the trigger, and decides whether it hit, where, and for how much. That is what makes it a shared world rather than a single-player mod.

What's been built

Guns first. Hitscan and projectile weapons with damage by range and hit location, aim down sights, recoil, sway and holding your breath, stances (crouch, prone, sprint, mantling over ledges), optics and thermal scopes, attachments, camos, akimbo, underbarrel launchers, tracers, a heartbeat sensor, a minimap radar, riot shields and thrown equipment. Every weapon, attachment and perk has a lore-friendly name in the world.

Kits, perks and killstreaks. Each character keeps five kits (guns, attachments, perks and equipment), edited at Quartermasters, class trainers and innkeepers. Perks and deathstreaks change how you fight; killstreaks were reimagined for Azeroth: a goblin guided rocket, a dwarven sentry turret, gnomish sky hornets, gryphon and wind rider gunships, siege bombers and a gyrocopter gunship. Everything unlocks through in-game achievements that reward playing every way.

Classes that answer guns. If everyone has a gun, what is a class for? Our answer: classes do what guns can't. Each class has three core abilities and six picks, one per tier, from three archetypes, so a rogue can be an assassin, a bard or anything in between. Spells are cast from your hands: ready a spell, charge it, hold it, and release it at the crosshair, with both hands for a stronger dual cast and ground-targeted area spells. Melee is in first person too, with whatever weapon you hold, and druids get their forms with bars of their own.

Companions. Collect companions that fight beside you, level up, learn moves, evolve (some of them into mounts), breed and trade. They can be downed and revived, just like you.

Construction. Build destructible structures anywhere in the open world, under rules that keep towns and quest hubs clear, or rent a protected homestead plot in one of the unfinished zones and build there in peace.

A more dangerous world. Creatures hear gunfire, call for help and chase harder; gunner camps shoot back; enemy tracers and damage-direction arcs show where fire is coming from; grenades bounce and can be cooked; the dead fall as ragdolls; and you loot every corpse around you in one window. Being downed instead of dying means your friends can get you back up.

The launcher and the site. One small exe installs itself, makes your account, downloads the game and fetches only what changed after that. If the game crashes, it offers to send the crash report, so we can fix what broke. womw.net shows the server's status live. In the game, Esc > Report Bug / Suggest sends a report straight to us, with where you were standing.

The first playtest

The first playtests, with friends, came in the first week of October, and on 12 October the server opened to playtesters through womw.net. Their notes shaped a lot of what came next: the second round fixed aiming on attachment variants, akimbo firing, the arm being cut off at the elbow, blueprints that failed to place, and equipment you couldn't use, and it added real flying rounds, rockets and grenade launchers, melee with your own weapon and much more. The in-game bug reports and crash reports exist because of that round. Thank you to everyone who played, broke things and told us about it.

Going open source

WOMW is about to become an open-source project. The code (the server's rules, the client layer, the launcher, the hosting scripts, this website and the design documents) will be public under the GNU GPL v3; the server keeps vmangos' GPL v2 and the client base keeps benilla's MIT/Apache license.

The repository will hold code only. It contains no game files and nothing read from them: the programs read your own installs. To play, you must own and have legal access to World of Warcraft and the original Call of Duty: Modern Warfare 2 (2009), and when you sign up you'll be asked to confirm exactly that.

Opening the code means you can see how it all works, report bugs with the source at hand, send fixes and features, and even host a server of your own for your friends.

What's next

Next up: the bounty board, more lore killstreaks, better first-person bodies, and a lot of polish from your reports. Follow along here, on Discord and on r/womw.
